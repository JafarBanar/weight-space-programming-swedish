\newcommand{\STwoText}{Round 1 tried 9 steering settings and found nothing (83.1\%). Round 2 searched 54: three ways of building the direction, six layers, three strengths, using 6,760 dev prompts.
Twenty-seven settings reached at least 95\% Swedish answers on the dev prompts, so \textbf{round 1 undersold steering}. The selected setting (S2) adds, after layer 6, the mean internal state of Swedish answers minus that of Danish answers.
On fresh data it cut wrong-language answers on new templates from 11 to 2 (C6 held, with the same caveat as C1: the baseline is below the gate) and raised Swedish answers on human-translated questions by 9.7 points $[+6.0,+13.7]$, more than any neuron edit.
It costs Danish too, less severely: Danish answers 47.3\%\up38.7\% and Danish perplexity $+36\%$. Steering is a run-time intervention, not a weight edit, and it consumed far more model feedback than the neuron recipe (6,760 against 160 dev prompts).}

\newcommand{\RThreeSecond}{\textbf{Second model, no retuning.} Every choice made on the 1.5B model (prompt, layer scaled by depth, strength, learning rates, epochs) was reused on \code{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct}; only the training targets and activation statistics were recomputed from that model. Its unedited rates are 67.3\% Swedish, 52.7\% Danish and 46.7\% Norwegian. For this weaker model G1 was pre-set as ``halves the wrong-language answers''. \textbf{LoRA distillation passes again}: 87.0\% Swedish (wrong-language answers 32.7\%\up13.0\%), 69.7\% Danish, 64.7\% Norwegian, 100\% English, no perplexity rise. The closed-form edit again raises all three languages (85.0 / 78.7 / 67.7) and again fails on quality, here perplexity ($+7.2\%$ Swedish, $+9.2\%$ Norwegian). The learned rank-3 edit helps less (75.0\%). The prompt chosen on the larger model raises Swedish answers (89.0\%) but lowers Danish by 5.7 points and fails G2: the wording did not transfer between models. The random edit has no effect here ($-0.3$ points).}
\newcommand{\RThreeDetector}{\textbf{Second language identifier.} All 55,150 saved answers of the three rounds were re-labelled with \code{langdetect}, which uses a different model and training data from the primary detector. The two agree on 92.3\% of answers; most disagreements are Danish against Norwegian, then Swedish against Norwegian. Across 169 test sets the right-language rate differs by 1.8 points on average. The conclusions hold with one caveat: under the second identifier F2 gives 89.7\% Swedish (269 of 300), just under the 90\% level it meets exactly under the primary one, and P3 gives 90.7\%. Danish and Norwegian rates are lower under the second identifier for every method, baseline included (F2: Danish 46.3\%\up72.3\%, Norwegian 53.3\%\up68.0\%), so the gains remain. The round-2 result is unchanged: 50.7\%\up50.7\% Swedish and 44.0\%\up10.0\% Danish for the neuron edit (\code{results/second\_detector.md}).}
